## 1. Features to Exclude (And Why)

Before selecting what to keep, we must eliminate columns based on three strict rules:

### A. Target Leakage (The Absolute "Must Drop")

* **`*Carbon intensity`**: $\text{PCF} = \text{Product Weight} \times \text{Carbon Intensity}$. In a real prediction scenario, you won't know `Carbon Intensity` until *after* you measure total emissions. Including this leads to fake 100% accuracy.
* **`Emissions at stage (kg CO2e)`** (from `stage_df`): Summing stage emissions equals total PCF.

### B. High-Noise / Unstructured Text & Identifiers

* **`*PCF-ID`**: Database primary key (866 unique values across 866 rows). Causes extreme overfitting.
* **`Product name`** (672 unique) & **`Product detail`** (496 unique): Unstructured free-text descriptions. For a small dataset (866 rows), converting text strings directly into categorical features adds hundreds of sparse, noisy columns.
* **`Company-reported reason for change`**, **`Relative change...`**, **`*Adjustments to raw data`**: Free-text commentary notes.

### C. Extreme Missingness

* **`*%Upstream estimated from %Operations`**: **100% missing (866 nulls)**. Cannot be used for training.
